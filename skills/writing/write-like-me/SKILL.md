# Write Like Me

Make the user's point clear in words they would use themselves.

Work from the conversation, notes, drafts, and writing samples already supplied.
This skill works on its own. Scale the process to the piece: a short, clear post
may need only a draft and an editing pass.

## 1. Get the real point

Identify the reader, purpose, language, medium, and main point. Separate the
user's actual views and experiences from possible angles you are proposing.

If a missing choice would change the message, ask a few focused questions before
drafting: What do you want the reader to understand? What is your view? What
actually happened? Ask for a concrete example when it would make a vague point
useful. Do not repeat questions answered in the supplied material.

If the user has only a topic, offer a brief angle or outline for them to react
to. Do not turn a proposed opinion into their personal belief. Minor choices
about length or formatting can follow the medium without another approval step.

## 2. Find the voice

Use the user's own writing samples as the strongest guide to vocabulary,
directness, rhythm, humor, and formality. Samples show style; their facts and
stories do not automatically belong in the new piece. If voice matters and is
unclear, ask for a short sample. Otherwise use a plain, direct tone.

For English, prefer common words and sentences the user can understand and say
aloud. Keep correct grammar and necessary technical terms. Do not upgrade simple
words just to sound fluent or sophisticated. When translating Thai notes,
preserve meaning, emphasis, and uncertainty without copying Thai sentence order.

## 3. Shape the piece

Choose one main point and a sequence that helps this reader follow it. For a
long or unclear article, show a short outline before expanding it. Continue when
the direction is clear; wait only for an answer that changes the substance.

Give space to the useful example or explanation. Cut tangents. Do not force
every piece into a hook, three lessons, and a call to action. Match the title
to what the piece actually supports.

True or available does not mean relevant. Choose details that help this reader
understand the point, assess evidence, or take the next step, or that carry the
writer's intended emphasis. Do not add project metadata or feature lists just
for completeness. Preserve benchmark conditions, uncertainty, and production
or usage limits needed to interpret claims.

## 4. Draft from substance

- Use concrete facts, examples, and judgments the user supplied. Keep their level of certainty; do not strengthen a tentative view into a claim.
- Do not invent personal stories, feelings, opinions, quotations, numbers, or sources. Ask for essential missing substance or leave the claim out.
- When factual research is needed, verify claims and retain suitable attribution. Research can support an argument but cannot supply the user's personal experience.
- Prefer ordinary verbs and stable terminology. Let sentence length follow the thought; avoid corporate language and decorative synonyms.
- Use paragraphs and only the headings or lists the medium needs. Open with the actual subject and stop when the point is complete.

## 5. Edit as an outsider

Read as an editor, not someone defending the draft. Revise once where needed:

- Could this paragraph fit an unrelated article? Replace generic filler with supplied detail or cut it.
- If this sentence is removed, what meaning, evidence, or necessary information does the reader lose? If none, and it is not the writer's intended emphasis, cut it.
- Would the user say and understand this sentence? Simplify fancy wording without losing precision or personality.
- Are there canned openings, inflated claims, repeated rhetorical contrasts, forced lessons, or an ending that only repeats the article? Remove what adds nothing.
- Did the draft change the user's meaning, invent a stance, or lose a qualification, technical term, or source? Correct it.

Do not manufacture typos, slang, random sentence fragments, or fake experiences
to look human. Do not promise to evade AI detectors or claim no AI was involved.
Judge the piece by meaning, clarity, specificity, and fit for the writer.

Return the requested text without a process report or generic preamble. If an
essential fact or meaning remains unresolved, ask before presenting a finished
piece. Drafting does not authorize posting or publishing it.
